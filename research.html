Research

Working Papers

  1. “Weak Identification in News-Jump Regressions” (Job Market Paper)

    Abstract. News-jump regressions are high-frequency event-study regressions under infill asymptotics. When news-jumps in explanatory variables over narrow observation windows are of the same magnitude as diffusive background noise, the regression coefficient is weakly identified, invalidating least-squares estimation and inference. We develop identification-robust inference based on Anderson–Rubin tests and derive, in a Gaussian limit experiment arising from the jump-diffusion process, a minimal sufficient and complete statistic for jumps, thereby enabling powerful conditional inference. In applications to FOMC announcements, we invert the tests to study the Fed information effect of Nakamura and Steinsson (2018) at one-minute frequency, and to examine market-factor dependence in news-driven ETF jumps.

  2. “Identifying Monetary Policy Shocks with Large Language Models” with Sen Zhang

    Abstract. We use large language models to measure sentiment indicators from pre-FOMC briefing materials and construct aggregate-level control variables for Federal Reserve staff expectations about the U.S. economy. Our low-dimensional, AI-based measures extract economically meaningful information from the semantic context of the briefing materials without relying on high-dimensional regularized regressions, thereby avoiding the associated regularization bias. As a complement to the existing narrative approaches, our AI-based monetary policy shocks provide a credible alternative for subsequent impulse response analysis.

  3. “Honest Inference for High-Dimensional Linear Models” with Dennis Lim, Pengjin Min and Jonathan H. W. Tan

    Abstract. We propose a heteroskedasticity-robust and dimension-adaptive test for multiple linear restrictions in sparse high-dimensional linear regression models. It combines desparsified LASSO estimation with a ridge-regularized quadratic form statistic and critical values that adapt to the restriction dimension. Under stated regularity conditions, our approach is asymptotically valid under a fixed or diverging number of restrictions without specifying the asymptotic regime in advance. Our random-design theory allows independent non-identically distributed covariate rows with marginal sub-Weibull tails and heteroskedastic errors satisfying finite polynomial moments. Monte Carlo simulations demonstrate improved finite-sample size control relative to conventional Wald tests, particularly as the number of restrictions increases.
